# Superstore Growth vs. Profitability Analysis

An executive dashboard analyzing the impact of discounting strategies on profit margins using Tableau Public and the Tableau Superstore dataset.

---

## Executive Summary
While overall revenue shows steady upward growth over time, aggressive promotional discounting in specific sub-categories severely erodes profitability. This visual report identifies where margin leakage occurs and establishes the relationship between high discount thresholds and net operating losses.

---

## Key Dashboard Views

* **The Macro Trend (Sales & Profit):** Dual-axis timeline tracking total sales volume against net profit to evaluate overall organizational growth and margin health.
* **Bleeding Categories (Where is the loss?):** Ranked horizontal bar chart highlighting high-performing product lines alongside unprofitable lines.
* **Root Cause (Discount vs. Profit):** Scatter plot mapping average discount rates against net profit across product sub-categories to isolate margin drivers.

---

## Key Takeaways & Insights

* **Targeted Margin Drain:** Overall sales continue to expand, but net profitability is heavily compromised by three bottom-tier sub-categories: **Tables**, **Bookcases**, and **Supplies**.
* **The 25% Discount Cliff:** **Tables (Furniture)** sold at an average discount exceeding **25%** operates at a sustained net loss of over **$17,000**, drastically offsetting gains made by profitable furniture lines like Chairs.
* **Strategic Imbalance:** High-margin sub-categories (e.g., Copiers, Phones, Accessories) maintain profitability through controlled promotional pricing, whereas deeply discounted furniture sub-categories fail to convert volume into net returns.

---

## Business Recommendations

1. **Cap Promotional Discounts:** Re-evaluate discounting tiers for the Furniture category, capping promotional allowances below 20% to stem net losses.
2. **Bundle Unprofitable Lines:** Repackage low-margin items (like Tables and Bookcases) with high-margin accessories rather than applying standalone price cuts.
3. **Margin-First Incentives:** Shift sales incentives from gross revenue targets to net margin contributions to discourage unprofitable volume selling.
